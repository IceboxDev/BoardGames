Chess Bughouse

Four players, two boards, two teams — and every piece you take goes to your partner.

The table

Two games of chess are played side by side, at the same time. You and your partner sit at different boards with opposite colours: if you are White on Board A, your partner is Black on Board B.

Board B · White

Your team wins as soon as either of you wins: the first checkmate, flag fall or resignation on either board ends the whole match.

Passing pieces

Whenever you capture a piece, it goes straight to your partner's hand (pocket) — the same colour as your partner's army. A captured piece that had been promoted from a pawn goes over as a pawn.

Dropping

On your turn, instead of moving a piece, you may drop one from your hand onto any empty square. Click it in your pocket, then click the square.

  • Pawns may not be dropped on the first or last rank.
  • A drop may give check — and even checkmate.
  • A drop may block a check.
  • A dropped pawn on its second rank may still move two squares.

Everything else is chess

Pieces move as in normal chess, including castling, en passant and promotion. Checkmate is decided by the pieces currently in hand. There are no draws by repetition or by the fifty-move rule, and there is no stalemate: a player with no legal move simply waits for a piece to arrive — while their clock runs.

Clocks

Bughouse is played fast: 3 minutes each is the standard here. Each board has its own pair of clocks; yours runs only on your turn.

Talking to your partner

The signal buttons tell your partner what you need — Need ♞, Need ♛… — or ask them to Sit! (stop moving, so the opponent can't get a piece) or Go! (play fast). Only your team sees them.

Bots

Solo, you play with three bots: a bot partner and two bot opponents, from Wanderer (random) to Shark (Fairy-Stockfish at full strength). Bots play their own board well but don't plan with their partner.
