> (card: [Zoology] Which of the following animals is capable of hibernating for up to three years?
A. Bat B. Bear C. Snail
Answer: C. The record for the longest hibernation is held by desert snails, which can seal themselves in their shells and go dormant for years, surviving even in museum collections.)
> notes: The card's 'up to three years' is a popular figure; the documented British Museum specimen (Eremina desertorum) revived after about four years, and in a 1904–1912 experiment some sealed snails survived about eight years. Snail dormancy in heat/drought is aestivation rather than hibernation. · handed off by nature/animals: Asks which animals survive years of dormancy sealed in their shells (desert snails, aestivation); dormancy states live in nature/ecology a1463 'Waiting Out the Cold: How Animals Switch Themselves Off', which already covers land snails' aestivation.
