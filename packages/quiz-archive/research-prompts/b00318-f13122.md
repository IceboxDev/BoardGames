## The question
Printed card (Trivial Pursuit, German) — our open wording: **Which member of the Beatles quietly added a high-pitched tone to "A Day in the Life" (the end of Sgt. Pepper's Lonely Hearts Club Band, 1967) so that his dog could enjoy it too?** Our current answer is **Paul McCartney**; we also accept John Lennon because accounts differ.

## The dilemma
- At the end of the UK LP (after the final piano chord of "A Day in the Life") there is a ~15 kHz tone, followed by the run-out-groove gibberish.
- Producer George Martin (e.g. in "Summer of Love: The Making of Sgt. Pepper", 1994) is often quoted crediting the idea to Paul McCartney ("Let's put on something which only a dog can hear").
- Ian MacDonald ("Revolution in the Head") and Wikipedia credit John Lennon with the idea.
- The "for his dog" motive (McCartney's sheepdog Martha) may be a popular embellishment; Lennon reportedly joked about dogs.
- Mark Lewisohn's "The Beatles Recording Sessions" (1988), based on EMI session documentation, and Geoff Emerick's memoir "Here, There and Everywhere" (2006) may give more authoritative accounts of the 21 April 1967 session where the tone and inner groove were recorded.

## What we need settled
- Whose idea the high-frequency tone was, according to the most authoritative sources (session documentation via Lewisohn, Emerick, Martin, interviews with McCartney or Lennon) — quote each with page numbers.
- Whether the "dog" motive is documented, and whose dog (if any).
- Whether the card's premise ("so that his dog could enjoy it") is fair, and how the question should be worded so it has one defensible answer — or whether the answer should be "accounts differ" with a stated main attribution.
