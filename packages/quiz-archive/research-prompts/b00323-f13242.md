## The question
Printed card (Trivial Pursuit, German): "Welche Lebenserwartung hat eine Termitenkönigin: 50 Stunden, 50 Monate oder 50 Jahre?" (What is the life expectancy of a termite queen: 50 hours, 50 months or 50 years?). Our open question asks: **Up to how many years can a termite queen live?** Our current answer is **up to 50 years**, with a caveat in the article that the Australian Museum documents up to 20 years in some species.

## The dilemma
- "Up to 50 years" is widely repeated in popular sources, but appears to be an estimate, possibly for large fungus-growing termites (Macrotermes) whose queens are rarely observed for their whole life.
- The Australian Museum states termite queens live up to about 20 years in some species.
- Laurent Keller's work (e.g. Keller 1998, "Queen lifespan and colony characteristics in ants and termites", Insectes Sociaux 45) compiled queen lifespans and is likely the primary source behind most figures; ant queens (Lasius niger) are documented up to 28–29 years in captivity.
- We must not teach a figure that rests only on popular repetition.

## What we need settled
- The longest documented or credibly estimated lifespan of a termite queen, by species, from primary literature (Keller 1998 and later studies; any long-term field observations of Macrotermes, Nasutitermes or other genera). Please quote the exact figures and whether they are measured, estimated or anecdotal.
- Where "50 years" originates, and whether any primary source supports it.
- Given the card's choices (50 hours / 50 months / 50 years), is "50 years" a defensible answer ("in the order of decades"), and how should the article phrase the documented maxima versus the popular claim?
