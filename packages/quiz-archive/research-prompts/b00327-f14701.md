## The question
Printed card (Trivial Pursuit, German): "Wie viele komplette Exemplare der 180 im 15. Jahrhundert gedruckten Gutenbergbibeln existieren heute noch: 11, 21 oder 31?" (How many complete copies of the 180 Gutenberg Bibles printed in the 15th century still exist today: 11, 21 or 31?). Our current answer is **21** (the card's apparent intended answer).

## The dilemma
- Wikipedia's lead says 49 copies survive at least substantially, of which 21 are complete.
- The Harry Ransom Center (University of Texas at Austin, owner of a complete copy) says 20 complete copies.
- Wikipedia's census section, and some other lists, arrive at 25 or other counts, depending on whether paper and vellum copies are counted alike, whether a copy missing a few leaves counts as complete, and whether multi-volume copies split between institutions count.
- Eric Marshall White's census ("Editio princeps: A History of the Gutenberg Bible", 2017) is regarded as the authoritative modern census; older censuses (e.g. Ilona Hubay; the Schwenke/De Ricci tradition) used different criteria.

## What we need settled
- According to the most authoritative current census, how many copies are complete (and what definition of "complete" it uses), split into vellum and paper copies.
- Exactly why the Ransom Center says 20, why Wikipedia says 21, and where 25 comes from — quote each.
- Also confirm the surviving total (48 or 49 substantial copies) and the print run estimate (158–180).
- Which answer a fair quiz should accept, given the card offered 11 / 21 / 31.
