## The question
Printed card (Trivial Pursuit, German): "Wie viele Seidenraupen werden zur Herstellung eines Kimonos benötigt: 20, 200 oder 2.000?" (How many silkworms are needed to make a kimono: 20, 200 or 2,000?). Our open question asks: **Roughly how many silkworms are needed to make one kimono?** Our current answer is **about 2,000**; the article notes that some estimates run several times higher.

## The dilemma
- A travel article (JAL Japan Travel, on Yūki silk weaving) gives about 2,000 cocoons.
- Wikipedia and other sources give figures around 3,000, 5,000 or even 9,000–10,000 silkworms/cocoons per kimono.
- The difference is probably about the weight of silk in a kimono (lightweight vs heavy formal kimono; one tan bolt of fabric ≈ 900–1,000 g of silk), the yield per cocoon (≈ 0.2–0.5 g of raw silk, filament length 800–1,500 m), and whether the figure counts cocoons, raw-silk weight or finished fabric.

## What we need settled
- Authoritative figures for cocoons/silkworms per tan (one bolt of kimono fabric) and per kimono from Japanese silk industry or research sources (e.g. the Dainippon Silk Foundation / 大日本蚕糸会, Japan Silk Center, NARO, Tomioka Silk Mill, Japanese textile museums, sericulture textbooks).
- The calculation behind the figures: grams of raw silk per cocoon, grams of silk per tan, cocoons per tan — with sources.
- Whether "about 2,000" (the card's intended choice among 20 / 200 / 2,000) is right in the order of magnitude, and what range the article should state.
